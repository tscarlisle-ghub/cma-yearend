Distributions Already Taken, 2026

Distributions are an equity draw, not a P&L expense — they never show up in Net Income above. But they DO get wired into the Plate 03 split below, explicitly, rather than being assumed away inside the starting bank balance. Here's why that matters: the bank balance nets these out at a flat 50/50 (that's how they were actually paid), but the year-end split is weighted by production — so if the split just used the post-distribution balance as-is, the flat 50/50 already taken would distort the weighted result. Methodology. Firm ending cash = starting bank balance − construction/move remaining + Scott's remaining income + Bill's remaining income − (monthly opex rate × months remaining) − combined staff bonuses − reserve for next year + landlord inflow. Because the April distributions were paid flat 50/50 but the split below is weighted by production, the model adds those distributions back into the pool before weighting it (so the weighted split isn't distorted by an already-flat payout), then subtracts each partner's own distribution back out — leaving each partner's true net-remaining entitlement, weighted by their YTD net income (shared/unattributed costs split evenly) plus remaining income, less that partner's own projected remaining expense run rate and bonus pool, less that partner's 50% share of depreciation still to be booked this year. Depreciation is deliberately excluded from the cash bridge above — it's a non-cash charge against Net Income, not an actual outflow, so it only affects the weighting, not ending firm cash.

This is a planning tool, not tax or accounting advice — reconcile final numbers with Todd Richard at Aprio before treating any figure here as a distribution decision. Figures are cash-basis and pre-tax.
